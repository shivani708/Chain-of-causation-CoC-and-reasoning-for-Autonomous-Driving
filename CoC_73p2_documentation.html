1. Overview

The model looks at the last four front-camera frames and writes one Chain-of-Caution (CoC) sentence: the driving decision followed by its reason, for example "Yield due to a pedestrian walking in the lane ahead." It can also answer free-form driving questions in the LingoQA style. It is a 2-billion-parameter vision-language model, so it runs in real time on one GPU.

2. Architecture

The model is Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct [1] with two LoRA adapters [5] merged into its weights. Qwen3-VL keeps the three-module design of Qwen2.5-VL [2]: a vision encoder, a vision-language merger that compresses image patches into tokens, and a Qwen3 [3] language decoder. After merging, the model is a plain Qwen3-VL checkpoint and needs no extra code.

3. Methodology

3.1 Data

  • Indian CoC data. 62,487 training samples from front-camera drives in Indian cities. Each sample is four frames plus one decision-and-reason sentence, in the Chain-of-Causation style of Alpamayo-R1 [4]. The Delhi and kota_out drives are held out and never trained on.
  • Object-detection pipeline behind the labels. YOLO [12] detects objects on 741,359 frames. Qwen3-VL-32B [1] then checks the objects, the road layout and the ego decision through vLLM [7], with rule-based consistency filters.
  • Open driving VQA. 24 public datasets listed by Qwen-Drive [10] were collected, including DriveLM [11] and nuScenes-based sets. They were used in earlier experiments (Section 6.3). WaymoQA [13] is test-only and was never trained on.
  • LingoQA [6] train questions were used for the RFT stage. The 500 LingoQA validation questions are only for scoring and were never trained on.

3.2 Stage 1: 68.6 model (coc_student_alpamayo)

Full fine-tune of Qwen3-VL-2B on the Indian CoC data in the Alpamayo CoC format, 3 epochs. LingoQA 68.6, WaymoQA 54.0.

3.3 Stage 2: RFT LoRA, 72.2 model (merged_rft_ck500)

Rejection-sampling fine-tuning (RFT) [9]: the 68.6 model trains on its own best answers.

  1. The 68.6 model sampled 8 answers for each of 10,000 LingoQA-train prompts (temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95).
  2. Lingo-Judge [6] scored every answer against the train reference. The mean pass rate was 49.5%.
  3. The best correct answer per prompt was kept: 8,490 answers (8,190 train, 300 validation), mean 27.9 words. Answers that just copied the literal text "action, justification" were removed.
  4. A LoRA was trained on these plus 3,000 CoC samples: lr 2e-5, warmup 30, global batch 16, 1 epoch, adapter saved every 100 steps.
  5. Step 500 scored 72.2 and was merged into the weights. All checkpoints from step 200 on scored 70.2–72.2.

3.4 Stage 3: speed-bump fix LoRA, 73.2 model (final)

Problem. In vehicle and video tests the 72.2 model said "slow down due to the speed bump" 33–57% of the time, mostly on yellow-black painted kerbs. 12.1% of the CoC training answers (7,554 of 62,487) mentioned a speed bump.

Label check. Qwen3.5-27B [3] was run through vLLM [7] on the four frames of each of those 7,554 samples, with a strict yes/no question. The question says that kerbs, dividers, barriers and flat zebra crossings are not speed bumps. Settings: temperature 0, thinking off, 8 tokens.

GroupnYES (bump visible)NOUnclear
Answers mentioning a speed bump7,55479 (1.0%)7,403 (98.0%)72 (1.0%)
Control: random "maintain" answers3000 (0%)297 (99.0%)3 (1.0%)

Cleaning. The 7,475 speed-bump samples without a confirmed bump were dropped. That leaves 55,012 CoC samples, of which 0.14% mention a speed bump (was 12.1%).

Training. A LoRA on the 72.2 model, trained on 16,000 random cleaned CoC samples plus the 8,190 RFT LingoQA answers (24,190 samples), keeps the LingoQA skill. Settings: r 64, α 128, lr 2e-5, warmup 30, batch 2 × 8 gradient accumulation, 1 epoch = 1,512 steps, saved every 150 steps. The best validation loss was 0.6654 at step 1500.

Selection. Every checkpoint was scored on LingoQA. Step 1500 scored highest (73.2) and was merged into merged_sbfix_ck1500.

4. Input pipeline

One user turn is [frame 1, frame 2, frame 3, frame 4, prompt], oldest frame first, in the Qwen chat template. The prompt must be exactly the training text:

These are 4 front-view camera frames captured by the ego vehicle in the seconds leading up to a driving decision, in time order. The last frame, frame 4, is the decision moment (t=0.0s).

You are given ONLY these frames -- no speed, trajectory or meta-action channel. Read the ego vehicle's own motion out of how the scene moves across the frames.

In one sentence, state what the ego vehicle did at the decision moment and why, on Indian roads: the driving decision, followed by the reason for it. Cite only what is already visible in these frames.

Frame sizefull frames; the processor resizes 1280×720 to ~704×384frames 1–3 at 256×144, frame 4 at 512×288
Speed-bump rate, Hyderabad1%0%
Speed-bump rate, test track10%17%

Only the first sentence of the answer is shown on screen. Some training answers repeat a tail such as "A mandatory speed limit sign is observed…", and that tail is cut off. The full answer is still written to the CSV log.

5. How to deploy

5.1 Install

python3 -m venv ~/coc_live && source ~/coc_live/bin/activate
pip install torch torchvision                    # CUDA build for the vehicle GPU
pip install -r deployment/requirements_live.txt  # transformers >= 5.0

5.2 Run in real time (one CoC line)

python3 deployment/live_coc_single.py                      # webcam 0
python3 deployment/live_coc_single.py --source 1           # second camera
python3 deployment/live_coc_single.py --source rtsp://<camera-ip>/stream
python3 deployment/live_coc_single.py --source drive.mp4 --no_display --out_video drive_coc.mp4

How the script works:

  • It keeps the last second of frames in memory.
  • When the model is free, it builds the 4-frame window ending at "now" and runs the model in a background thread, so the video never freezes.
  • It draws CoC: <decision + reason> on the video.
  • Every answer is logged to live_coc_answers.csv.
  • Keys: p pauses, q quits.

5.3 Question answering mode

python3 deployment/live_camera_vqa.py --question "Is it safe to overtake now?" --ask_every 10

5.4 Hardware

  • NVIDIA GPU. 0.4–0.6 s per answer on an A40 in bf16. The weights alone are 4.26 GB.
  • CPU only. Several seconds per answer, so the display text lags behind the scene.

All commands, including server evaluation and rebuilding the model, are in COMMANDS.txt.

The model was run live with the autonomous stack on the campus testbed. 230 CoC answers over about 17 minutes. The reference clip reference_clip/testbed_coc_5min_210s-510s.mp4 is the 5-minute stretch from 3:30 to 8:30 with the most scenario types.

Correct:

  • pedestrians crossing (yield, stop)
  • cross traffic (stop)

  • motorcycles and cyclists
  • cones

  • a red traffic light
  • clear road ("Maintain lane because of clear path ahead")

Wrong: about 25% of answers still said "speed bump". Only one stretch of the drive had a painted bump, at about 10:00 in the full screencast. The model also sometimes said "sharp curve" on straight road.

7. Limitations and the test-track experiment

  • On roads unlike the training cities (the TiHAN test track and campus roads), yellow-black kerbs and road-edge markings still sometimes produce "speed bump" or "sharp curve".
  • The model reads motion only from the four frames. It has no speed, steering or map input.
  • Answers are one sentence and advisory only.

Test-track experiment (lora_ttfix_v1). Qwen3.5-27B labelled 1,362 windows from the two clean test-track recordings. The "maintain" labels were capped at 400 windows, so the model would not learn a single answer. A LoRA was trained on these labels plus cleaned CoC and LingoQA data. It was tested on 116 s of test track held out from training:

The experiment fixed the test track but lowered LingoQA. It also made the model less cautious in the city: it missed "slow down for the curve" at chevron-marked bends. The 73.2 model was therefore kept as the final model. The next step is clean camera recordings (no overlays) of the campus route, labelled the same way.
